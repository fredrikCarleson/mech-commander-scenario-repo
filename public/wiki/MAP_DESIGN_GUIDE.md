## Core principle

> Evergreen map-design ideals for Meridian Strike.

A good tactical mech map needs four things:

1. Open fire lanes
2. Cover and line-of-sight blockers
3. Movement obstacles
4. Valuable positions worth fighting over

A bad map is either:

- **Too open:** long-range units dominate
- **Too dense:** movement becomes slow and frustrating
- **Too flat:** positioning does not matter
- **Too random:** tactics feel accidental

---

# Recommended terrain ratios

For a balanced standard map, try to aim for these proportions:

| Terrain type                     | Recommended share | Purpose                                        |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------: | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Plain / open ground              |        **45–60%** | Movement, fire lanes, maneuver space           |
| Forest / cover                   |        **15–25%** | Defensive positioning, ambush, approach routes |
| Rough / rocks / rubble           |         **8–15%** | Slows movement, creates route decisions        |
| Elevation / hills / ridges       |        **10–20%** | Tactical advantage, line of sight, objectives  |
| Water / marsh / pools            |         **3–10%** | Chokepoints, cooling zones, risk/reward        |
| Impassable / cliffs / deep water |          **0–8%** | Shape the battlefield, but use sparingly       |
| Objectives / special zones       |          **1–5%** | Focus conflict and movement                    |

# Map Archetypes

Instead of a scattered random layout, create **map archetypes** that teach a different tactical lesson.

## 1. Balanced battlefield

Best for standard battles.

- Medium mechs can maneuver freely.
- Long-range weapons get clear lanes.
- Short-range mechs can use forests and ridges to approach safely.
- Gives the player several viable tactical plans.

## 2. Long-range duel map

Best for fire-support battles.

- High ground matters heavily.
- Long-range weapons shine.
- Fast scouts can spot and flank.
- Cover exists, but does not dominate.
- **Important:** Never make it completely open. Add 2–3 safe approach routes.

## 3. Ambush / forest map

Best for close-range brawls and scouting.

- Line of sight is broken frequently.
- Short-range units can easily close the distance.
- Scouts become incredibly valuable.
- Enemies can hide or flank.
- **Warning:** If forest coverage goes above 35–40%, the map may become slow and messy.

## 4. Canyon / chokepoint map

Best for defense missions.

- Movement routes matter.
- Heavy mechs can anchor chokepoints.
- Fast mechs can flank through side paths.
- Missiles and indirect fire become highly useful.
- **Important:** Always provide at least 2–3 viable routes, never just one corridor.

## 5. Wetlands / cooling map

Best if heat management is central to the mission.

- Water offers cooling but slows movement.
- Players can risk exposure for heat management.
- Heavy energy-weapon mechs face interesting choices.
- Water should not just block movement—it should create a **temptation**.

## 6. Lunar / low-cover map

Best for brutal open combat.

- Fewer hiding places.
- Craters and ridges replace forests as primary cover.
- Movement and range control matter more.
- Damage is much more punishing.

# The most important design rule: terrain must form shapes

Do **not** scatter terrain randomly hex by hex (e.g. forest, plain, forest, plain, rock).

Great maps are made from **features**, not individual tiles.

- Forest clusters
- Ridge lines
- River curves
- Crater fields
- Rocky outcrops
- Open valleys

The map should first have large shapes, then assign hex terrain within those shapes.

---

# Tactical ingredients every good map should have

## 1. At least two major fire lanes

A fire lane is a line where long-range weapons can matter.

- **Recommended:** 2–4 major fire lanes per map, roughly 8-15 hexes long and 2-4 hexes wide.
- Never make all fire lanes point directly from spawn to spawn. Use diagonal lanes, central valleys, or ridge-to-ridge angles.

## 2. At least two covered approach routes

Short-range mechs need ways to close.

- **Recommended:** 2–3 covered approach routes using forest, ridge shadows, rough terrain, or craters.
- A good player should be able to think: “I can get my brawler close if I move through the woods and stay behind the ridge.”

## 3. High ground that is useful but risky

High ground should give an advantage, but not be free.

- Good high ground offers better line of sight and firing angles, but is exposed from multiple directions, slower to reach, and vulnerable to flanking.
- Do not place one perfect hill directly in the player spawn. That removes the decision.

## 4. Chokepoints, but never only one

Chokepoints create tactical drama, but one single chokepoint creates boredom.

- **Recommended:** 2–3 chokepoints, each with at least one alternate route.
- Good pattern: Main road through a valley, a rough side path, a risky water crossing, and a high ridge flank.

## 5. Objectives should pull players into danger

Never put objectives in safe corners.

- Good objective placement should be near the contested center, partly exposed, reachable by multiple routes, but threatened from others.
- **Recommended:** Place primary objectives 45–60% across the map from the player spawn. Extraction zones should rarely be the same as the starting spawn.

# Spawn and engagement distances

For a standard 15×15 map:

- **Player and enemy should usually start 9–13 cells apart.**
- **Too close:** No maneuvering; an immediate brawl.
- **Too far:** Slow opening turns; long-range units dominate without contest.

A good tactical map should usually create its first meaningful contact around **Round 2 or 3**, rather than immediately on round 1 (unless it is a designated ambush mission).

---

# Map quality checklist

A good map should answer “yes” to most of these questions:

- [ ] Can a fast mech flank?
- [ ] Can a heavy mech anchor a position?
- [ ] Can a long-range mech find useful fire lanes?
- [ ] Can a short-range mech approach without suicide?
- [ ] Is there at least one risky but rewarding position?
- [ ] Are there at least two routes to the objective?
- [ ] Does terrain break line of sight without making the map cramped?
- [ ] Can the player recover from a bad first move?
- [ ] Does the map create meaningful choices by round 2?
- [ ] Would the map play differently with a different lance composition?

If yes, the map is ready for the community repository!
