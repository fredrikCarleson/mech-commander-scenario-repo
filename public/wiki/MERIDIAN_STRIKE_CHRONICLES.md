Meridian Strike — Deployment Chronicles

This account describes the campaign setting and intended successful arc. A live
company may retry, withdraw or accept a failed nonfinal contract. Its settled
results and service records remain authoritative for losses and achievements.

OP-101 — Border Signal

Meridian Strike began with four battle-scarred machines, six pilots, and a company ledger that left little room for failure. The hangar lights stayed on only because Mara Voss accepted contracts other commanders considered too small, too remote, or too uncertain.

The first assignment appeared routine: investigate a communications blackout along the Meridian border and remove any hostile presence interfering with the sector beacon. Local authorities blamed smugglers or raiders. Mara expected something more disciplined. The jamming pattern was deliberate, the approach routes had been watched, and the attackers had chosen positions meant to test whoever came looking.

Beyond the temperate treeline, NEEDLE-class scouts were waiting around the compromised signal equipment.

For Meridian Strike, the mission offered badly needed credits and a chance to prove the company could operate as more than a collection of survivors. Yet somewhere inside the static, an unknown commander was already listening.

Before the first shot had been fired, Meridian Strike had entered someone else’s campaign.

Narrative function: Call to adventure; the company takes its first contract and enters VEX’s shadow.

OP-202 — Silent Relay

The fighting at Border Signal revealed that the jamming was not an isolated raid. Hidden inside the ruined equipment, Tala Orin recovered fragments of encrypted telemetry leading away from the planet and toward a dormant relay station on a scarred moon.

Someone had been observing the border through a network that official records claimed was inactive.

The relay’s data core might identify the force behind the attacks, but extraction would require Meridian Strike to occupy the station and hold it while the download completed. The lunar surface offered little cover, no breathable atmosphere, and no easy way to disperse combat heat. Every movement would be visible against the pale crater fields.

Then a voice entered the company channel.

COMMANDER VEX spoke as though Meridian Strike had already become familiar to him. He knew the company’s name. He knew its machines had survived the border engagement. His tone carried neither anger nor surprise—only interest.

Mara understood the warning. Meridian Strike was no longer investigating the enemy network.

The network had begun investigating them.

Narrative function: Crossing the threshold; the company discovers that the war is organized and personal.

OP-303 — Hold the Line

The active, defended relay deepened Mara’s suspicion. VEX commanded more than scattered border raiders. The station linked the border interference to an observation network. A completed download could reveal its traffic, but control of the station alone did not prove what every file contained.

VEX directed the company's attention toward its repair traffic. Mara treated this as a threat rather than proof that he knew the base's location. The incoming assault made the next danger concrete, even for a company that had failed to secure the relay.

His retaliation came against the company’s forward staging base, where fuel reserves, repair equipment, ammunition, and wounded personnel had been gathered behind a narrow desert pass. If the base fell, Meridian Strike would lose more than a contract. It would lose the infrastructure keeping its damaged machines operational and its pilots alive.

The canyon offered one advantage: the enemy could not bring its full strength forward at once. It also offered one danger: once committed, the defenders would have nowhere to withdraw without exposing the base.

Mara ordered the company into the pass.

This was no longer a reconnaissance contract or an intelligence recovery. Meridian Strike would have to hold against a commander who had studied its limits—and intended to break them.

Narrative function: Road of trials; VEX tests whether the young company can endure sustained pressure.

OP-404 — Cut the Head

Meridian Strike survived VEX’s assault, but the defense consumed armor, ammunition, and time the company could not easily replace. The enemy had demonstrated that as long as VEX controlled the theater network, every base, convoy, and repair schedule remained vulnerable.

The relay intelligence finally produced a possible answer.

SIGINT traced a concentration of command traffic to an industrial badland where VEX appeared to be directing operations through a heavily protected command chassis designated VEX-PRIME. Destroying it could disrupt the hostile network before another major offensive began.

Mara knew the risk. A commander who watched every movement might also choose what his enemies were permitted to see. Yet waiting would surrender the initiative and allow VEX to dictate the next battlefield.

Meridian Strike prepared for a direct strike through defensive screens guarding the command position. The mission offered no safe approach and little margin for prolonged combat.

For the first time, the company was not responding to VEX’s actions.

It was hunting him.

Whether the target was truly the head of the enemy—or merely another layer of the trap—remained unknown.

Narrative function: Approach to the inmost cave; Meridian attempts to seize the initiative and confront VEX directly.

OP-505 — Deep Extract

The strike against VEX-PRIME did not end the campaign.

VEX-PRIME was a working field command node, not verified proof of the human commander's location. Destroying it disrupted a real position without eliminating every hostile force. Hunter machines still threatened the routes behind the line.

Deep Extract sent the company through an extraction corridor near an old supply depot. The hunters were positioned to deny the approach to its evacuation point. There was no confirmed captive whose rescue would restore a named pilot to the roster.

The evacuation window required at least one living company machine at the extraction hex in round seven. Defeating all hostile machines could also complete the operation. Darius wanted to cover every possible return; Sera and Jonas warned that covering an exit was useful only while it remained usable.

Mara kept the objective and the company's duty to its people in the same conversation.

Meridian Strike had survived because its pilots believed they would not be discarded when the ledger turned against them. That belief was worth more than any machine, but only if the company proved it under fire.

Assault chassis were not authorized for this operation. The deployed force needed speed, discipline and enough firepower to keep the route usable.

VEX expected Meridian Strike to protect its assets.

Instead, the company worked to turn attachment into information and mutual support. The result was the actual fate of its deployed people and machines, not an automatic promise that every member came home.

Narrative function: Supreme ordeal; the company chooses loyalty over mercenary expediency.

OP-606 — VEX: Last Stand

Meridian Strike entered the final operation carrying the history of every battle that had brought it there.

The company had encountered VEX in the border static, investigated his relay network, faced his assault, pursued his field command node and attempted extraction through his hunters. Individual results could include accepted losses. Each operation added to the company's actual history; none guaranteed a clean return. The final objective was to destroy the hostile lance controlling the basin.

VEX had withdrawn into a fortified desert basin prepared as a final kill zone. Electronic warfare blanketed the approaches. Heavy machines guarded the chokepoints. His personal lance waited where every route could be observed and every mistake punished.

There would be no larger force arriving to finish the war. No employer would assume responsibility for the outcome. Meridian Strike would enter the basin with the pilots, machines, and scars it had earned.

VEX believed a mercenary company was only a ledger of expendable assets.

Mara intended to prove that a company could become something more—and that its name could survive the commander who tried to erase it.

Narrative function: Apotheosis and ultimate boon; Meridian confronts its shadow and earns its identity.
